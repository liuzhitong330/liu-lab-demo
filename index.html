Motivation Target gate LNP screen Relevance

The Liu Lab couples intracranial convection-enhanced delivery with CRISPR screens and single-cell readouts to find glioblastoma vulnerabilities that depend on treatment and tumor context. Its next delivery problem is especially interesting: the best CRISPRoff target is only useful if an LNP can carry the mRNA and sgRNA into enough tumor cells without losing the relevant biological signal.

I built a replicate-aware target gate over the lab's public CRISPRi screen and connected it to the lab's newer human GBM43 CED Perturb-seq analysis. It turns two public datasets into a shortlist for payload-bearing LNP tests, then lays out the controls and readouts needed to tell a delivery failure from a biological failure.

How it works

The 2024 GL261 screen reports three gene-level phenotypes across two biological replicates: untreated growth (gamma), growth under radiation (tau), and the radiation-normalized effect (rho). Negative rho values nominate perturbations that make cells less fit specifically under radiation. The first analysis applies transparent thresholds to all 10,252 genes; the second asks whether the same target was modeled in the lab's 18,938-cell human GBM43 CED dataset and converts that evidence into a two-stage LNP screen.

What this lets the lab do: distinguish formulation delivery, target engagement, and treatment-dependent biology before committing to a survival cohort.

Where Cathy adds value: I translated the screen evidence into an experiment with a non-targeting payload, benchmark LNP, payload-free control, IVIS, RT-qPCR or methylation, guide capture, and an explicit go/no-go decision.

The recommendation changes with the target and with the minimum editing fraction. A formulation that produces a strong reporter signal but misses the editing floor should return to formulation or payload optimization; it should not be interpreted as evidence that the biological target failed.

Why this matters for the lab

PRKDC is a useful test case because it is the strongest radiation-sensitizing hit in the GL261 screen (mean rho −1.97; −2.27 and −1.68 across replicates) and it reappears in the human GBM43 CED analysis as one of only three RT-dependent perturbations. That does not prove an LNP–CRISPRoff therapy will work, but it gives the delivery experiment a stronger biological anchor and a clear failure tree.

What I would bring

I would bring hands-on mouse GBM, IVIS, tissue collection, cell culture, RT-qPCR, flow cytometry, and assay-documentation experience alongside Python and R analysis. That combination is useful here because formulation QC, intracranial execution, target engagement, single-cell readouts, and computational interpretation have to stay connected.

Methods note

This is a processed-data analysis of the authors' open supplementary workbooks plus a structured extension of the executed Liu Lab notebook at commit 53c8ccf. Primary sources: Liu et al. 2024, the GBM43 Zenodo record, the 2026 GBM43 preprint, and the intracerebral MC3 benchmark. The genome-scale data are mouse GL261 and in vitro; the human GBM43 study is a preprint; CRISPRi and CRISPRoff are not interchangeable; and no public Berkeley formulation measurements were available. LNP labels, QC gates, and the two-stage design are proposals, not Liu Lab SOPs.
